## Key Findings

### 1. CRT pipeline is the bottleneck at all scales
- 4x: CRT ≈ 56-58% CPU
- 6x: CRT ≈ 77-78% CPU  
- 8x: CRT ≈ 83-84% CPU

### 2. WinForms vs Avalonia CRT profile differs
- **WinForms**: `Crt_Render` and `Curvature` show up as separate hot lambdas (explicit Parallel.For at each stage)
- **Avalonia**: `Parallel.ForWorker` inner frame dominates because TPL inlines user body into its own scheduler frame. `Curvature` shrinks because its work appears inside ForWorker samples
- Net pipeline cost is similar — just measurement attribution differs

### 3. Curvature scales worst at 8x (WinForms)
- `ApplyFullFrameCurvatureAndConvergence` jumps to **49.8%** at 8x WinForms
- This is per-pixel math + nested Parallel.For; highest ROI for SIMD

### 4. Emulation core shrinks fast
- WinForms 4x → 8x: 28.2% → 8.7% (−70% relative)
- Any future emulation-core optimization has dwindling impact at high scales

### 5. Avalonia's Release + TieredPGO wins at 4x (+10%)
- Tier-1 codegen for `ppu_step_new` (11.9% vs 15.0%) shows up in emulation-core savings
- Delta narrows at 6x/8x because CRT (which doesn't benefit as much from Tier-1) crowds out emulation

---

## Strategic Takeaways

1. **To raise 8x FPS** → SIMD-vectorize `ApplyFullFrameCurvatureAndConvergence` and `Crt_Render` inner loops, or reduce per-pixel work (lower internal scale, upscale at output)
2. **Don't touch emulation core at 6x/8x** — it's < 10% of CPU budget
3. **WinForms upgrade to .NET 8+** would close most of the 4x gap (TieredPGO), less impact at higher scales
4. **Audio mode 2 (`GenerateWaveform` 2.8% at 4x, falls to 0.9% at 8x)** — cheap, keep as default
